Car prices · IBM course project

Can we predict a car's price
from its specs?

A car dealer wants a fair price for each car. I tested four regression models on engine size, horsepower, weight and fuel economy, picked the best one, and checked honestly how far the result can be trusted.

The short version

Problem, work, answer

The whole project in 30 seconds.

01 / problem

Price a car fairly

"If we know a car's engine size, horsepower, weight and fuel economy, can we predict its price?"

02 / work

Four models, one test set

I found the features most related to price, kept 40 cars aside as a test set, and trained linear, multiple, polynomial and Ridge regression.

03 / answer

Yes, with a warning

Ridge explains 85% of the price with an average error of about 2,070 USD. But cross-validation shows the model is weak for car brands it has not seen.

DATAThe UCI Automobile dataset, cleaned version from IBM's Data Analysis with Python course: 197 cars after removing missing values. Prices are in US dollars from 1985, so they are not today's prices. This was the course's final assignment, rewritten as a clean notebook.

What I learned

Four lessons from this project

Each one is backed by a result above.

More features help. Six features explain much more of the price than horsepower alone (R² 0.82 vs 0.62).

More complex is not always better. A polynomial of degree 5 scored lower than degree 3. That is overfitting.

Ridge controls overfitting when there are many polynomial features. It gave the best score.

One test split can look too good. Cross-validation gave 0.57 instead of 0.82, and showed the missing brand feature.

LIMITSSmall (197 cars) and old (1985 prices) data. Only numeric features are used, so brand and body style are missing. The Ridge alpha was chosen on the test set; a cleaner way is cross-validation on the training set. Next step: add the brand as a feature.

Process

How I did it

Python (pandas, scikit-learn, Matplotlib, Seaborn) in a Jupyter notebook.

step_01

Clean

Removed extra index columns, kept only numeric columns and dropped rows with missing values: 197 cars left.

step_02

Explore

Checked the price distribution, the correlation of each column with price, and the price by brand.

step_03

Train

Kept 20% (40 cars) as a test set. Trained linear, multiple linear, polynomial (degree 1 to 5) and Ridge (6 alpha values).

step_04

Compare & check

Compared R² and average error in USD, then used 5-fold cross-validation to see if the score holds.
